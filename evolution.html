Theory

The evolution of rock

Darwin published On the Origin of Species almost a century before Chuck Berry, yet he described well what happened to rock. There is variation, there is inheritance and there is an environment that favors some traits and discards others.

Bands learn from records much as descendants inherit genes. They copy badly, and the error becomes novelty. Audiences, radio and money choose what continues. Hence the tree.

Twelve mechanisms

Inheritance

Common ancestor

Every species descends from another. The hardcore scream and the arena ballad seem to come from different planets, yet followed back through their lineages they reach the same blues.

BluesRhythm and BluesRock and Roll
Variation

Mutation

Evolution needs errors. Fuzz was born from torn speakers and burnt amps, and metal's low tuning came from Tony Iommi's factory accident. One day's defect became a genre's main trait.

Garage RockHeavy MetalShoegaze / Dream Pop
Environment

Natural selection

Radio, labels, clubs, laws and wallets filter what survives. Classic rock is largely what withstood FM programming. Good music that finds no niche vanishes, while ordinary music in the right place multiplies.

Classic RockArena Rock / AORPost-Grunge
Geography

Speciation by isolation

Separated populations diverge. Rainy, remote Seattle produced grunge, cold Norway the second wave of black metal, and postwar Germany krautrock. Darwin saw the same in the Galápagos, with finches.

GrungeBlack MetalKrautrock
Explosion

Adaptive radiation

When a group finds empty niches it diversifies fast. Between 1965 and 1970 psychedelia, prog, hard rock and metal appeared almost at once, and the multitrack studio was the newly found continent.

Psychedelic RockProgressive RockHard Rock
Tempo

Punctuated equilibrium

Stephen Jay Gould proposed that long stable periods are broken by sudden change. Rock has its jumps: 1955, 1967, 1977, 1991. Within months, what had been the center turned into a fossil.

Punk RockGrungeRock and Roll
Hybridization

Horizontal gene transfer

Bacteria swap genes without needing descent. So do genres. Rap rock absorbed hip-hop at once, ska punk brought in Jamaica and Tropicália swallowed the guitar in the name of cultural cannibalism. That is why this tree has lines that cross.

Rap Rock / Rap MetalSka PunkTropicália
Competition

Arms race

In the Red Queen hypothesis, you must keep running just to stay in place. Thrash, death, grindcore and deathcore speed up and tune lower with each generation to keep sounding extreme to already accustomed ears.

Thrash MetalDeath MetalGrindcoreDeathcore
Display

Sexual selection

A peacock's tail hinders flight but wins mates. Makeup, platforms, hair and pyrotechnics follow the same logic. In glam, display became the main function and is often worth more than the riff.

Glam RockGlam MetalSymphonic Metal
Founders

Founder effect

A few individuals colonize a place and carry only part of the variation. BRock was born from a handful of British records in Brasília. That early isolation explains why the same genes became another creature.

Brazilian Rock (BRock)Beat / British InvasionLatin Rock
Return

Atavism and revival

Ancestral traits sometimes reappear. Britpop revived the 1960s, stoner the 1970s and The Strokes brought garage back. In music, unlike biology, the dodo can return if someone finds the record.

BritpopStoner RockRockabilly
Loss

Vestigial organs

Cave fish lose their eyes because they do not need them. Nu metal lost the guitar solo, post-rock lost the chorus and sometimes the voice, and krautrock lost the blues. Evolving also means letting go.

Nu MetalPost-RockKrautrock

Where the metaphor ends

  • Genres are not species. Bands can belong to several at once, which is impossible for an animal.
  • In music, evolution is intentional: artists choose what to copy. That is closer to Lamarck than to Darwin.
  • Genre genealogies are debated. Dates and branches on this tree show the most accepted origin, not a single truth.
  • "Evolving" does not mean improving. Djent is not superior to blues, just as a human is not superior to an insect: both are individuals with their own particularities.
