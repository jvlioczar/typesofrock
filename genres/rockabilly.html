Rockabilly

Crossover / fusion of Country and Rhythm and Blues
Type Crossover / fusionEmerged 1954Habitat Sun Records, MemphisClade Trunk
Country → Rockabilly
The environment

Context

It was the direct cross between young white Southerners and the R&B they listened to on the sly. At Sun Studio in Memphis, Sam Phillips was looking for someone who could sing like a Black artist, and Elvis arrived in 1954 with "That's All Right".

The phenotype

Sound

Slapped upright bass, clean guitar with tape echo, a hiccuping voice and almost no drums on the first recordings. It sounds nimble, dry and full of space.

The mutation

What sets it apart

It parts from broader rock'n'roll through dominant country DNA and slapback echo. It was a short but hardy lineage: it returned with the Stray Cats in the 1980s and lives on in niche scenes like a living fossil.
